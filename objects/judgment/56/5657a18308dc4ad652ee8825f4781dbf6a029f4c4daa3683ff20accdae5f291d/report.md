## `bssc-sum-capacity/two-letter-marton-two-symbol-pruning`

**Verdict: Valid, conditional on the theorem explicitly assumed in the claim.**

### 1. Exhaustion of supports

There are exactly six two-element subsets of \(\{00,01,10,11\}\):

- four Hamming-distance-one adjacent pairs;
- \(\{01,10\}\);
- \(\{00,11\}\).

Singleton support carries no information because the channel input is constant. Thus the case split covers every induced super-input support of cardinality at most two.

### 2. Adjacent pairs

For an adjacent pair, one input coordinate is constant and the other is binary and varies. Under \(P^{\otimes2}\), the output generated by the constant coordinate is independent of the varying input and supplies no information.

For the varying coordinate, the following joint coupling preserves the two specified receiver marginals:

- under input \(0\), \((Y,Z)=(0,0)\) or \((1,0)\), each with probability \(1/2\);
- under input \(1\), \((Y,Z)=(1,0)\) or \((1,1)\), each with probability \(1/2\).

The pair \((Y,Z)\) is therefore a \(\operatorname{BEC}(1/2)\) observation of the input. Because private-message achievability depends only on the separate marginals, this coupling may be selected for a converse. Allowing the receivers to cooperate then produces a point-to-point \(\operatorname{BEC}(1/2)\), whose capacity is \(1/2\) bit. Hence every adjacent-support Marton sum rate is at most \(1/2<0.615\).

The exact transition-row checks in `verify.py` corroborate the claimed adjacent-pair structures.

### 3. Antirepetition support \(\{01,10\}\)

For each receiver marginal, the two inputs induce:

- one common output with probability \(1/2\), serving as an erasure;
- one input-identifying output with probability \(1/2\).

Thus each marginal is a relabeled \(\operatorname{BEC}(1/2)\). The two marginals can be coupled to use the same erasure event. Cooperation again gives a point-to-point \(\operatorname{BEC}(1/2)\), so every Marton rate on this support is at most \(1/2<0.615\).

The exact arithmetic in the checker verifies the required row structure.

### 4. Repetition support \(\{00,11\}\)

This restriction is a binary-input broadcast channel, so the theorem assumed in the claim applies: its Marton sum-rate supremum equals its randomized-time-division supremum.

Writing \(q=\Pr[S=00]\), the \(Y^2\) mutual information is correctly computed as

\[
J(q)=H_2\!\left(\frac q4,\frac q4,\frac q4,1-\frac{3q}{4}\right)-2q,
\]

because \(00\) produces the uniform four-output distribution while \(11\) produces a point mass. By reflection, \(I(S;Z^2)=J(1-q)\).

For an RTD law with \(q_w=\Pr[S=00\mid W=w]\) and \(\bar q=\mathbb E q_W\), the common-layer term satisfies

\[
\min\{I(W;Y^2),I(W;Z^2)\}
\le \frac{I(W;Y^2)+I(W;Z^2)}2.
\]

Using the Markov relation \(W-S-(Y^2,Z^2)\),

\[
I(W;Y^2)=J(\bar q)-\mathbb E J(q_W),
\]

and analogously for \(Z^2\). Consequently the submission’s bound

\[
M\le
\frac{J(\bar q)+J(1-\bar q)}2
+\frac12\mathbb E|J(q_W)-J(1-q_W)|
\]

is correct. Concavity of mutual information in the input distribution implies that \(J\) is concave, so the reflection-symmetric concave function \(J(q)+J(1-q)\) is maximized at \(q=1/2\). Therefore

\[
M\le J(1/2)+\frac12\max_q|J(q)-J(1-q)|.
\]

The derivatives are correctly calculated:

\[
J'(q)=\frac34\log_2\frac{4-3q}{q}-2,\qquad
J''(q)=-\frac3{\ln2\,q(4-3q)}.
\]

For \(D(q)=J(1-q)-J(q)\),

\[
D''(q)=-\frac3{\ln2}
\left(
\frac1{(1-q)(1+3q)}
-\frac1{q(4-3q)}
\right)<0
\]

for \(q>1/2\), since the relevant denominator comparison differs by \(2q-1>0\). Together with \(D(1/2)=D(1)=0\), this establishes \(D\ge0\) on \([1/2,1]\) and reduces the absolute-value maximum to \(\max D\) there.

At \(q_0=17/20\), concavity gives the global tangent upper bound. The pinned execution reports the rigorous outward intervals

\[
J(1/2)<0.548794940695399,
\]

\[
D(17/20)<0.130566822139301,\qquad D'(17/20)<0,
\]

and

\[
\max D<0.131083799962750.
\]

Thus

\[
M<0.614336840676774<0.615.
\]

The interval implementation uses directed arithmetic; its logarithm enclosures, positive-denominator divisions, tangent maximization, and strict endpoint comparisons are consistent with the mathematical argument.

### 5. Consequence for two-letter improvements

The problem record identifies the one-letter RTD value as beginning

\[
0.361642884421954615663\ldots,
\]

so twice this value begins

\[
0.723285768843909231326\ldots
>0.7232857688439092.

Independent product use supplies the corresponding two-letter Marton witness. Any strict two-letter improvement over the current per-use achievable rate must therefore have total two-letter Marton sum exceeding this baseline. Since every law supported on at most two super-input symbols has sum below \(0.615\), such an improving law must assign positive probability to at least three symbols.

This argument makes no assertion about three- or four-symbol supports.
