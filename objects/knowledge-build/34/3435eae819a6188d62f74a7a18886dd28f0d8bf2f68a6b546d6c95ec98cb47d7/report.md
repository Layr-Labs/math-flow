## Node: program/uniform-cyclic-half-intervals

- **Title:** Uniform cyclic half-interval replicability
- **Type:** Research program
- **Parent:** `root`
- **Status:** Active

For odd \(k\), let \(X=\mathbb Z_k\), and let \(H_k\) be the \(k\) cyclic translates of an interval of length \((k-1)/2\). This program studies learning every target in \(H_k\) when the unlabeled distribution is fixed to be uniform on \(\mathbb Z_k\).

Write

\[
n_{\mathrm{unif}}(H_k,\varepsilon,\delta,\rho)
\]

for the least sample size required in this fixed-distribution experiment by a learner that must be accurate and replicable for every target in \(H_k\).

According to primary judgment
`sha256:f026796afeef5d30732d0e6b4b985b9275ed5c05003a85faf8d59480f870913c`,
the complete current program state is:

1. **Proper upper bound.** There is a proper learner with sufficient sample size

   \[
   n\ge
   \max\left\{
   \frac1\varepsilon\log\frac2\delta,\,
   \frac{12}{\varepsilon\rho}
   \right\}.
   \]

2. **Lower bound against unrestricted learners.** If

   \[
   0<\varepsilon\le\frac14,\qquad
   0<\delta,\rho\le\frac1{20},\qquad
   k\ge\frac1{5\varepsilon\rho},
   \]

   then every randomized, computationally unbounded, possibly improper learner that works for all targets under the uniform distribution requires

   \[
   n\ge\frac1{120\varepsilon\rho}.
   \]

3. **Qualified characterization.** These bounds yield

   \[
   n_{\mathrm{unif}}
   =\Theta\!\left(\frac1{\varepsilon\rho}\right)
   \]

   for fixed sufficiently small confidence parameters, and more generally when

   \[
   \log(2/\delta)=O(1/\rho).
   \]

4. **Worst-case implication.** The fixed-uniform lower bound transfers to the global quantity \(n_{\mathrm{rep}}\), but only as the weak lower bound \(1/(120\varepsilon\rho)\), with no growing \(\log k\) factor.

5. **Alphabet-size limitation.** The program establishes that the fixed uniform one-coordinate mechanism does not generate an additional growing \(\log k\) cost. It does not establish the same conclusion for adversarial nonuniform distributions on \(\mathbb Z_k\).

6. **Tensorization lead.** A coordinatewise calculation suggests a \(d^2/(\varepsilon\rho)\) sample scale for a naive multidimensional construction, but the primary judgment classifies this only as a heuristic.

This is a distribution-restricted program. It contains no arbitrary-distribution upper bound for \(H_k\), no lower bound with logarithmic exponent greater than \(3/2\), and no universal learner improving the global exponent or the established \(\rho^{-2}\) dependence.

## Node: program/uniform-cyclic-half-intervals/proper-upper-bound

- **Title:** Proper replicable upper bound under the uniform distribution
- **Type:** Result
- **Parent:** `program/uniform-cyclic-half-intervals`

\[
L=\left\lfloor\frac{\varepsilon k}{2}\right\rfloor+1

\[
q=
\left\lceil
\frac{k}{\lfloor\varepsilon k/2\rfloor+1}

\[
n\ge
\max\left\{
\frac1\varepsilon\log\frac2\delta,\,
\frac{4q}{\rho}
\right\}.

The primary judgment accepts the simplification

\[
q<\frac2\varepsilon+1\le\frac3\varepsilon,

\[

\max\left\{
\frac1\varepsilon\log\frac2\delta,\,

This is an upper bound only for the fixed uniform distribution. It is not an arbitrary-distribution learner for \(H_k\).

## Node: program/uniform-cyclic-half-intervals/uniform-all-learners-lower-bound

- **Title:** Uniform-distribution lower bound against unrestricted learners
- **Type:** Result
- **Parent:** `program/uniform-cyclic-half-intervals`
- **Status:** Supported as proven by the primary judgment

For odd \(k\), suppose

\[
0<\varepsilon\le\frac14,\qquad
0<\delta,\rho\le\frac1{20},\qquad
k\ge\frac1{5\varepsilon\rho}.
\]

The primary judgment finds that every randomized, computationally unbounded, possibly improper learner that is \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-accurate and \(\rho\)-replicable for every target \(h_i\in H_k\) under the fixed uniform distribution on \(\mathbb Z_k\) requires

\[
n\ge\frac1{120\varepsilon\rho}.
\]

The lower bound therefore covers the unrestricted learner model from the original problem; its restriction is the fixed uniform distribution, not properness, computational efficiency, or determinism.

The primary judgment validates the supplied proof structure as follows:

- Replicability is converted into concentration of each fixed-target, fixed-seed output distribution around a selected mode.
- Accuracy implies that these modes are accurate on average over targets and seeds.
- A symmetric, doubly stochastic coupling between nearby targets preserves the correct target/sample marginals while producing identical labeled inputs on coupled transitions.
- Replicability consequently forces neighboring target-dependent modes to agree with high probability.
- Accuracy for sufficiently separated endpoint targets forces at least one mode change along a long path.
- Combining the mode-agreement and path-change estimates yields the stated lower bound.

The judgment expressly finds that the argument applies to randomized and improper learners and does not make an invalid selection of a single favorable seed.

## Node: program/uniform-cyclic-half-intervals/coordinatewise-tensorization-heuristic

- **Title:** Naive coordinatewise tensorization scaling
- **Type:** Heuristic method
- **Parent:** `program/uniform-cyclic-half-intervals`
- **Status:** Plausible lead; not a theorem

The primary judgment records the following coordinatewise scaling observation.

If a \(d\)-coordinate construction effectively supplied about \(n/d\) samples to each coordinate, applying the one-coordinate boundary-instability estimate would suggest per-coordinate instability of order

\[
\frac{d}{n\varepsilon}.
\]

A union bound over all \(d\) coordinates would then suggest total mismatch of order

\[
\frac{d^2}{n\varepsilon},
\]

and a naive coordinatewise sample scale of order

\[
\frac{d^2}{\varepsilon\rho}.
\]

The primary judgment qualifies this calculation as a heuristic rather than a multidimensional theorem. In the original multidimensional i.i.d. setting:

- coordinate sample counts are random rather than deterministically equal to \(n/d\);
- some coordinates may receive substantially different numbers of observations; and
- a global learner may exploit structure not captured by independent coordinatewise learning.

Consequently, the calculation establishes neither an upper bound nor a lower bound for the original multidimensional family. It remains only a possible guide for studying tensorized constructions.
