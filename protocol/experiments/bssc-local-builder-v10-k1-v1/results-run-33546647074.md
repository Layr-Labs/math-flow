## Diagnosis

The V10 minimal-accounting prompt overcorrected the earlier K2 over-expansion.
It repeatedly says to minimize program cardinality and that a dependency-ordered
theorem chain serving one portfolio decision belongs to one program. That rule
was useful at K2, where the general product-additivity theorem and its BSSC
specialization really do serve one tensorization work package. At K1 it causes a
category error: dependency among accepted results is treated as evidence that
their downstream remaining-work policies are inseparable.

The two K1 leaves share a narrow parent but expose different future intervention
surfaces. Evidence can solve or prune the fixed-cardinality reduction question
without settling whether channel-specific consistency excludes the witness, and
vice versa. The result-dependency edge should therefore not collapse the program
boundary.

## Recommended next candidate

Keep the successful K2 controls against one-program-per-result, but replace the
dependency-chain shortcut with a two-pass rule:

1. Separate or consolidate intermediate results according to semantic reuse and
   mathematical dependency.
2. Independently identify the future remaining-work packages that those results
   open, close, or redirect. Split programs when a plausible future contribution
   could independently zero, prune, stop, or materially re-estimate one such
   package, even when the current results occur in one proof chain or share a
   portfolio-level objective. Record mathematical dependency only on the result
   graph unless downstream pursuit policies are genuinely inseparable.

An additional useful prompt question is: "What distinct future contribution
types would receive local credit under this node?" If two contribution types can
change disjoint remaining-work quantities, they require separate leaf programs
under their narrowest shared activation parent.

The next experiment should repeat K1 with that targeted distinction. K2 should
then be rerun as a regression gate to verify that its two results remain in one
program. No retry of this exact candidate is warranted because all stages
accepted the same semantically coherent but accounting-wrong interpretation.
