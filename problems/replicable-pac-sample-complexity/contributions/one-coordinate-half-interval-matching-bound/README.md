# One-coordinate cyclic half-intervals have matching uniform-distribution bounds

## Exact restricted theorem and quantifiers

Let \(k\geq 3\) be odd, let \(X=\mathbb Z_k\), and put

\[
\ell=\frac{k-1}{2},\qquad
I_i=\{i,i+1,\ldots,i+\ell-1\}\pmod k,
\qquad h_i=\mathbf 1_{I_i}.
\]

Write \(H_k=\{h_i:i\in\mathbb Z_k\}\), and fix \(D\) to be the uniform
distribution on \(\mathbb Z_k\).  This is one coordinate of the cyclic
half-interval construction of Larsen--Mathiasen--Pabbaraju--Svendsen.

The following result is **distribution-restricted**: \(D\) is fixed to be
uniform.  The upper bound is proper, while the lower bound allows every
randomized, computationally unbounded, possibly improper learner.

**Theorem.**

1. For every odd \(k\geq3\) and every
   \(\varepsilon,\delta,\rho\in(0,1)\), define
   \[
   L=\left\lfloor\frac{\varepsilon k}{2}\right\rfloor+1,
   \qquad q=\left\lceil\frac{k}{L}\right\rceil.
   \]
   There is a proper randomized learner \(A_{k,\varepsilon}\) such that, for
   every target \(h_i\in H_k\), \(A_{k,\varepsilon}\) is simultaneously
   \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-accurate and \(\rho\)-replicable under \(D\) whenever
   \[
   n\geq
   \max\left\{
      \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{2}{\delta},
      \frac{4q}{\rho}
   \right\}.
   \]
   Since \(q<2/\varepsilon+1\leq3/\varepsilon\), it suffices that
   \[
   n\geq
   \max\left\{
      \frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{2}{\delta},
      \frac{12}{\varepsilon\rho}
   \right\}.
   \]

2. Conversely, suppose
   \[
   0<\varepsilon\leq\frac14,qquad
   0<\delta,\rho\leq\frac1{20},qquad
   k\geq\frac{1}{5\varepsilon\rho}.
   \]
   If a randomized, possibly improper learner \(A\), using \(n\) samples, is
   \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)-accurate and \(\rho\)-replicable under the fixed
   uniform \(D\) for **every** target \(h_i\in H_k\), then
   \[
   n\geq\frac{1}{120\varepsilon\rho}.
   \]

The order of quantifiers in the lower bound is therefore

\[
\forall A\;\forall n\;
\left[
  \left(\forall i\;\Pr_{S,r}[\operatorname{err}_i(A(S;r))>
  \varepsilon]\leq\delta\right)
  \land
  \left(\forall i\;\Pr_{S_1,S_2,r}[A(S_1;r)=A(S_2;r)]
  \geq1-\rho\right)
\right]
\Longrightarrow n\geq\frac{1}{120\varepsilon\rho}.
\]

Here each \(S,S_1,S_2\) is independently drawn from the realizable labeled
distribution determined by the already quantified target \(h_i\), and the two
runs in the replicability probability share the seed \(r\).  The distribution
\(D\) is not chosen after the learner: it is the fixed uniform distribution.

Thus, in the lower-bound regime
\(0<\varepsilon\leq1/4\), \(0<\delta,\rho\leq1/20\), and sufficiently large
\(k\), the restricted sample complexity is
\(\Theta(1/(\varepsilon\rho))\), with no dependence on
\(\log |H_k|=\log k\).

## Half-interval geometry

For \(0\leq t\leq\ell\), direct inspection of the two shifted half-intervals
gives

\[
\Pr_{x\sim D}[h_i(x)\ne h_{i+t}(x)]=\frac{2t}{k}.
\tag{1}
\]

The same formula holds for \(i-t\).  Moreover, along either orientation the
disagreement sets are nested: if \(0\leq t\leq s\leq\ell\), then

\[
\{x:h_i(x)\ne h_{i+t}(x)\}
\subseteq
\{x:h_i(x)\ne h_{i+s}(x)\},
\tag{2}
\]

and likewise with \(+t,+s\) replaced by \(-t,-s\).  These two elementary facts
drive both bounds.

## Upper bound

### The learner

Fix a partition of the cycle \(\mathbb Z_k\) into \(q\) nonempty consecutive
blocks, each of length at most \(L\), and distinguish one representative in
each block.  The shared random seed is a uniform shift \(U\in\mathbb Z_k\) of
this entire partition, including its representatives.

For a labeled sample \(S\) generated by target \(h_i\), let

\[
V(S)=\{j\in\mathbb Z_k:h_j\text{ is consistent with }S\}
\]

be the version space.  It is nonempty because \(i\in V(S)\).  The learner does
the following.

- If \(V(S)\) is contained in one shifted block, output that block's fixed
  representative hypothesis.
- Otherwise, output the least-indexed member of \(V(S)\) in the fixed numeric
  representation \(\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\).

Both outputs belong to \(H_k\), so the learner is proper.

### Accuracy

If \(V(S)\) is contained in one block, that block contains the target \(i\), and
its representative is at circular distance at most \(L-1\) from \(i\).  By
(1), its error is at most

\[
\frac{2(L-1)}{k}
=\frac{2\lfloor\varepsilon k/2\rfloor}{k}
\leq\varepsilon.
\]

In the fallback case the learner outputs a consistent hypothesis.  A
consistent hypothesis with error greater than \(\varepsilon\) has circular
distance at least

\[
L=\left\lfloor\frac{\varepsilon k}{2}\right\rfloor+1
\]

from \(i\).  If \(L>\ell\), no such hypothesis exists.  Otherwise, nesting (2)
shows that the existence of any such consistent hypothesis implies that at
least one of \(h_{i+L}\) and \(h_{i-L}\) is consistent.  Each of these events
has probability \((1-2L/k)^n\).  Hence

\[
\Pr_{S,U}[\operatorname{err}_i(A(S;U))>\varepsilon]
\leq 2\left(1-\frac{2L}{k}\right)^n
\leq 2e^{-2Ln/k}
\leq2e^{-\varepsilon n}.
\tag{3}
\]

This is at most \(\delta\) under the first displayed sample-size condition.

### Replicability

Let

\[
R(S)=\max_{j\in V(S)}d_{\mathrm{cyc}}(i,j).
\]

For every \(1\leq t\leq\ell\), nesting (2) and a union bound over the two
orientations give

\[
\Pr[R(S)\geq t]
\leq2\left(1-\frac{2t}{k}\right)^n
\leq2e^{-2nt/k}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\mathbb E R(S)
\leq2\sum_{t\geq1}e^{-2nt/k}
=\frac{2}{e^{2n/k}-1}
\leq\frac{k}{n}.
\tag{4}
\]

View a block boundary as a cut edge of the cycle.  The base partition has
exactly \(q\) cut edges.  After a uniform shift, each fixed cycle edge is a cut
with probability \(q/k\).  The union of the shortest paths from \(i\) to all
members of \(V(S)\) contains at most \(2R(S)\) edges.  If none is cut, the whole
version space lies in the shifted block containing \(i\).  Thus, by a union
bound and (4),

\[
\Pr_{S,U}[V(S)\text{ is not contained in the block containing }i]
\leq\frac{2q}{k}\mathbb E R(S)
\leq\frac{2q}{n}.
\tag{5}
\]

For two independent samples with the same \(U\), if neither bad event in (5)
occurs, both version spaces lie in the same block containing \(i\), and both
runs output the same representative.  Therefore

\[
\Pr_{S_1,S_2,U}[A(S_1;U)\ne A(S_2;U)]
\leq\frac{4q}{n}
\leq\rho.
\]

This proves the upper bound.

## Lower bound

The proof keeps the learner, seed, samples, target, and distribution quantifiers
separate.  Its key device is a symmetric, sample-dependent coupling that makes
two differently targeted labeled data sets exactly equal while preserving both
target/sample marginals.

### Modes from accuracy and replicability

For target \(i\) and fixed internal seed \(r\), let \(\mu_{i,r}\) be the output
distribution of \(A\) over a fresh sample, let

\[
p_{i,r}=\max_f\mu_{i,r}(f),
\]

and choose a canonical maximizer \(m_r(i)\).  This mode exists even for an
improper learner because \(X\) is finite, so the classifier range
\(\{0,1\}^{X}\) is finite.  If

\[
c_{i,r}=\sum_f\mu_{i,r}(f)^2
\]

is the fixed-seed collision probability, then \(c_{i,r}\leq p_{i,r}\).
Replicability therefore implies, for every \(i\),

\[
\mathbb E_r[1-p_{i,r}]\leq
\mathbb E_r[1-c_{i,r}]\leq\rho.
\tag{6}
\]

Let \(a_{i,r}\) be the fixed-seed probability that the learner's output has
error greater than \(\varepsilon\).  If the mode \(m_r(i)\) itself has error
greater than \(\varepsilon\), then \(a_{i,r}\geq p_{i,r}\), and hence

\[
\mathbf1\{\operatorname{err}_i(m_r(i))>\varepsilon\}
\leq a_{i,r}+1-p_{i,r}.
\]

Averaging over a uniform \(i\in\mathbb Z_k\) and the seed gives

\[
\Pr_{i,r}[\operatorname{err}_i(m_r(i))>\varepsilon]
\leq\delta+\rho.
\tag{7}
\]

No seed has been selected or moved across a target quantifier here.

### An exact symmetric coupling at scale \(M\)

If \(n\geq k/24\), the assumed lower bound on \(k\) immediately yields

\[
n\geq\frac{k}{24}\geq\frac{1}{120\varepsilon\rho}.
\]

It remains to consider \(1\leq n<k/24\).  (The case \(n=0\) is already
impossible: two targets at mutual error greater than \(2\varepsilon\) have
disjoint \(\varepsilon\)-accuracy events, while a zero-sample learner has the
same output law for both and \(\delta<1/2\).)

Set

\[
M=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{12n}\right\rfloor.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{k}{24n}\leq M\leq\frac{k}{12n}\leq\frac{k}{12}.
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(S=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\sim D^n\) be an unlabeled sample and write
\(T_i(S)=((x_j,h_i(x_j)))_{j=1}^n\).  For a fixed target \(i\), the probability
that \(T_i(S)=T_{i+M}(S)\) is

\[
\alpha=\left(1-\frac{2M}{k}\right)^n
\geq1-\frac{2nM}{k}
\geq\frac56.
\tag{9}
\]

Put \(\gamma=1/(3\alpha)\leq2/5\).  Conditional on \(S\), define a Markov
kernel on target indices by

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_S(i,i+M)&=\gamma\,
 \mathbf1\{T_i(S)=T_{i+M}(S)\},\\
K_S(i,i-M)&=\gamma\,
 \mathbf1\{T_i(S)=T_{i-M}(S)\},\\
K_S(i,i)&=1-K_S(i,i+M)-K_S(i,i-M).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The diagonal entry is nonnegative because \(2\gamma\leq4/5\).  The kernel is
symmetric: availability of the move \(i\to i+M\) is exactly availability of
the reverse move.  It is therefore doubly stochastic.

Now draw \(i\) uniformly, independently draw \(S\sim D^n\), and draw \(v\)
from \(K_S(i,\cdot)\).  Symmetry gives the following exact properties.

1. Conditional on every fixed \(S\), \(v\) is uniform.  Hence \(v\) and \(S\)
   are independent with the correct target/sample marginal.
2. Whenever \(K_S(i,v)>0\), the labeled data sets are identical:
   \(T_i(S)=T_v(S)\).
3. By (9)--(10), conditional on every \(i\), after averaging over \(S\),
   \[
   v-i\ \text{is uniform on }\{-M,0,M\}.
   \tag{11}
   \]

Fixing the learner seed \(r\), identical labeled inputs produce identical
outputs.  If \(m_r(i)\ne m_r(v)\), at least one of those two identical outputs
is not its corresponding mode.  Using both correct marginals and (6) therefore
gives

\[
\Pr_{i,v,r}[m_r(i)\ne m_r(v)]\leq2\rho.
\tag{12}
\]

Let

\[
b=\Pr_{i,r}[m_r(i)\ne m_r(i+M)].
\]

Translation invariance makes the analogous probability for \(-M\) equal to
\(b\).  Combining (11) and (12) yields

\[
\frac{2b}{3}\leq2\rho,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad b\leq3\rho.
\tag{13}
\]

### A long mode-constant path is impossible

Take

\[
t=\left\lfloor\frac{\varepsilon k}{M}\right\rfloor+1.
\]

Then

\[
\varepsilon k<tM\leq\varepsilon k+M
\leq\frac{k}{4}+\frac{k}{12}<\frac{k}{2}.
\tag{14}
\]

By (1), the targets \(h_i\) and \(h_{i+tM}\) have mutual error
\(2tM/k>2\varepsilon\).  Thus their modes cannot be equal if both modes have
error at most \(\varepsilon\): the triangle inequality for disagreement would
give mutual error at most \(2\varepsilon\).

If none of the \(t\) consecutive mode edges

\[
i\to i+M\to\cdots\to i+tM
\]

changes value, the endpoint modes are equal.  A union bound, (7), and the fact
that every translated edge start remains uniform therefore give

\[
1\leq2(\delta+\rho)+tb.
\tag{15}
\]

From (8),

\[
t\leq\frac{\varepsilon k}{M}+1\leq24\varepsilon n+1.
\tag{16}
\]

Combining (13)--(16),

\[
1-2\delta-2\rho
\leq3\rho(24\varepsilon n+1),
\]

and hence

\[
n\geq\frac{1-2\delta-5\rho}{72\varepsilon\rho}.
\tag{17}
\]

For \(\delta,\rho\leq1/20\), the numerator in (17) is at least \(13/20\),
which is larger than \(72/120\).  Therefore
\(n\geq1/(120\varepsilon\rho)\), completing the lower bound.

## What this says about the open exponent

This theorem isolates the one-coordinate statistical mechanism and shows that
it costs \(\Theta(1/(\varepsilon\rho))\), not a power of \(\log k\).  Merely
increasing the alphabet size \(k=|H_k|\) therefore cannot produce the open
\((\log|H|)^c\) lower-bound exponent.

Because a learner for the canonical worst-case problem must in particular work
for this uniform distribution, the restricted lower bound does imply the valid
worst-case corollary

\[
n_{\mathrm{rep}}(k,\varepsilon,\delta,\rho)
\geq \frac{1}{120\varepsilon\rho}
\]

in the stated parameter range.  Its point is negative with respect to the open
exponent: this corollary has no growing factor in \(\log k\).

It also explains why direct coordinatewise tensorization is lossy.  In a
stratified \(d\)-coordinate version with \(n/d\) samples per coordinate, (5)
is of order \(d/(n\varepsilon)\) per coordinate.  Requiring exact equality of
the full \(d\)-coordinate output and applying a union bound incurs another
factor \(d\), suggesting \(n=O(d^2/(\varepsilon\rho))\) for this naive method.
This scaling observation is **not** claimed as a theorem for the original
i.i.d. \(d\)-coordinate instance.  The known family-specific
\(\widetilde O((\log|H|)^{3/2}/(\varepsilon\rho))\) learner uses more global
structure; recovering that improvement is precisely where a one-coordinate
argument stops scaling.

- The new theorem, proof, and exposition were produced by an OpenAI Codex Math
  Flow solver agent in this session; no human authorship is inferred.  The
  proof uses only the displayed elementary half-interval geometry, collision
  probabilities, and a symmetric coupling; no external theorem is imported.
- The canonical Math Flow ledger had no prior contribution or research
  direction for this problem when this artifact was prepared.
- The fixed-uniform-\(D\) restriction is essential to the claim.  The theorem
  does **not** supply a learner for arbitrary \(D\).  Its lower bound transfers
  to the worst-case definition as just noted, but only at order
  \(1/(\varepsilon\rho)\), with no \(\log k\) gain.
- The result is only for one coordinate.  It neither improves the headline
  exponent beyond \(3/2\) nor improves the known universal upper bound.
- The lower-bound family size is allowed to depend on
  \(\varepsilon,\rho\) through \(k\geq1/(5\varepsilon\rho)\); this dependence is
  explicit.
- This is a proof-only artifact with no computational evidence and no
  objective-verification request.
- No research direction was registered, updated, or completed.
